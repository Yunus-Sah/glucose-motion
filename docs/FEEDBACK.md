# How the feedback works

## Workout numbers

- **Start:** the first available reading during the workout.
- **End:** the last available reading during the workout.
- **Change:** end minus start.
- **Minimum and maximum:** the lowest and highest readings during the workout.
- **Average:** the sum of those readings divided by the number of readings.

The first and last readings may not be taken at the exact times you pressed Start and End.

## Comparing A and B

Each session shows its date, activity and time between the meal and workout. If a matching meal is saved for the same profile, the app measures this time from the end of the meal. If only a manually entered meal time is available, it is labelled as the meal start. The app does not guess a missing meal time.

The workouts are lined up at their own start, shown as minute zero:

- Blue line: session A.
- Purple line: session B.
- Orange markers: meal times.
- Teal shaded area: the period when both sessions were still exercising.

The graph can show readings before and after exercise. The numbers in the table use readings during exercise only.

## Three parts of the feedback

### During training

The app describes whether glucose ended higher or lower than it started, and by how much. It can also describe a fall early in the workout followed by an overall rise, when the readings show this pattern.

### After training

The app compares the last reading during exercise with the last available reading in the next 60 minutes. It states how many minutes of data were available after the workout.

To compare the drops after two workouts, both need at least 45 minutes of data. Their last readings must be no more than five minutes apart in time since the workout ended. Otherwise, the app explains that the available data are too different for this comparison.

### What this means

The app describes which meal-to-workout interval went together with the observed pattern. It does not claim that meal timing caused the pattern or recommend when or how much to eat.

## Reading the result carefully

A change after exercise is an endpoint difference. It is not a speed of change or the difference between the highest and lowest readings. It does not measure performance or diagnose a condition. Other differences between the two days may also affect glucose.

The graph uses the original times. Lines stop when there is a gap longer than ten minutes. The app does not invent missing readings. The feedback has not been medically validated.
