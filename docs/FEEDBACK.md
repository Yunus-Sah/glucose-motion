# How feedback works

## Exercise statistics

Start and end are the first and last available readings inside the logged exercise interval. Change is end minus start. Minimum, maximum and arithmetic mean use those same readings. These endpoints can differ from glucose at the exact button-press times.

## Context and comparison

A and B are identified by date, activity and recorded meal-to-workout delay. When a matching meal event exists for the same profile, the delay is measured from meal finish. A manually entered meal time is explicitly labelled as meal start. No timing is inferred when the reference is missing.

Both charts are aligned to each workout's start, minute zero. Blue is A; purple is B. Orange markers identify meal references. The teal band shows the interval when both were exercising. Before/after context can appear in the graph; the statistics table covers exercise only.

## Athlete feedback

- During training: direction and size of the endpoint change. An early fall followed by a positive net change is described when supported by the readings.
- After training: change from the last exercise reading to the last available reading within 60 minutes after exercise, with the actual follow-up duration stated.
- Interpretation: identifies the observed association with meal timing without claiming causation or recommending a fueling strategy. Comparing follow-up drops requires at least 45 minutes for each session and endpoints no more than five minutes apart; otherwise the coverage limitation is reported.

A follow-up change is not a rate, peak-to-trough excursion, diagnosis or performance measure. Differences across two days can reflect many factors besides meal timing.

The graph retains original timestamps and breaks lines across gaps longer than ten minutes. Missing observations are not invented. Clinical validity has not been established.
